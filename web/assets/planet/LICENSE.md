# Earth data and procedural weather

`bright-stars.bin` contains measured J2000 directions, visual magnitudes and B−V colours for 8,404 objects with positions and V ≤ 6.5 in the Yale Bright Star Catalogue, 5th Revised Edition (D. Hoffleit and W. H. Warren, Jr., 1991). The data are packed from NASA HEASARC's BSC5P table. Missing B−V is rendered neutral. The ride assumes local sidereal time 02:48 at departure and advances the sky on Earth's sidereal clock. This is a fictional crossing without a dated ephemeris. The screen response and the passenger's adaptation time are modeled.

The integrated light between the catalogue stars comes from Karl D. Gordon’s first-iteration blue and red Pioneer 10/11 IPP coadds. The redistributed file is a one-degree grid of two surface-brightness measurements in S10(G2V). Where the observations have a gap, the reconstruction approaches the median at the same Galactic latitude. The visual sensitivity, colour balance and screen luminance are modeled. The original measurements have had their bright stars removed. [Pioneer coadds and calibration](https://karllark.github.io/data_pioneer10_11_ipp.html) · [Galactic photometry analysis](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202451086)

[NASA HEASARC catalogue and attribution](https://heasarc.gsfc.nasa.gov/W3Browse/all/bsc5p.html) · [CDS V/50](https://cdsarc.cds.unistra.fr/viz-bin/cat/V/50)



`earth-day.webp`: NASA Earth Observatory Blue Marble Next Generation, August 2004; 5400x2700 source downsampled to 4096x2048, WebP quality93. Geographic surface albedo only.

Source/credits: https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/blue-marble-next-generation/base-map/



`earth-night.webp`: NASA Earth Observatory Black Marble2016, 3600x1800 geographic night-light composite, WebP quality92. Based on Suomi-NPP/VIIRS observations. Not live lights or current population data.

Source/credits: https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/earth-at-night/maps/

`cloud-noise-64.rgba`: original deterministic periodic 64x64x64 RGBA8 3-D numerical noise, seed74019; X fastest then Y then Z, channels FBM / inverted Worley8 / inverted Worley16 / smooth broad noise. Generated by qa/planet/build_noise.py. This is scalar density data, not an image of clouds or a sky view.



All live cloud forms, motion, occlusion and shadows are calculated in 3-D. No RGB cloud radiance, view image or sky panorama is loaded by planet.js. The NASA global grayscale cloud composite `earth-cloud-density.webp` supplies a scalar initial weather-density field at orbital distances; its geography is advected/deformed and lit by the live solar model. Close clouds are procedural 3-D volumes. Orbital density blends in across 30–285 km of altitude. The live per-camera volume pass runs at half linear resolution and is reconstructed into the final view. It is not a pre-rendered scene.



The regional Atlantic volume uses archived NOAA GOES-19 ABI measurements acquired July 1, 2025 at 15:00 UTC. `atlantic-weather.png` stores optical depth, cloud-top height and retrieval coverage over 57°W to 15°W and 15°S to 15°N. Exact source objects, checksums and bounds are in `regional-sources.json`. [NOAA optical-depth product](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/metadata/geoportal/rest/metadata/item/gov.noaa.ncdc%3AC01508/html) · [NOAA open data distribution](https://registry.opendata.aws/noaa-goes/)

World coordinates: +X east,+Y up,+Z north; Earth radius6371km, cloud bands0.7-3.5,4.6-12.1,10.8-13.6km; spherical atmosphere ceiling120km. Sun direction, weather seconds, wind and coverage come from shared journey metrics. The real-time scattering model is an approximation, not an observational radiance simulator.





Cloud-density source: https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/the-blue-marble-true-color-global-imagery-at-1km-resolution/



Layer structure follows NASA atmospheric layer descriptions and NOAA cloud classification. Marine streets, localized deep convection/anvil outflow and separately sheared thin cirrus are approximate procedural weather, not a forecast. Exponential Rayleigh (8km) and aerosol (1.2km) scale heights, a broad Chappuis ozone absorber centered25km and a restrained nightside90km airglow layer model visibly different altitude regimes. The modeled squall line carries rain to 50 m and overshooting convection reaches 15.55 km. Atmosphere becomes negligible through the upper120km computational boundary; thermosphere/exosphere particle simulation is outside this renderer.

References: https://www.nasa.gov/image-article/earths-atmospheric-layers-3/

Rendering is an artistic real-time radiance approximation. The geographic maps are observed data; procedural cloud microstructure, ozone RGB coefficients and airglow brightness are modeled, not measured radiometry. Native Blender uses equivalent node noise rather than the identical browser LUT.
