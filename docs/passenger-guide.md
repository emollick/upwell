# Passenger guide

Board Car 07 at a working equatorial ocean terminal. A six-minute passage carries the observation saloon, crew sleeper and service lock up a physical guideway to the nonrotating dock at Aster Reach. The O’Neill cylinder turns beyond the dock.

## Play

From the observation saloon, look through the glass as the boarding bridge withdraws. The water around Pelagic Anchor serves the tug *Nereid*, the supply vessel *Ceiba* and *Pilot 12*. A moored cable workshop carries full and empty reels beneath its lifting frame. A fixed cable depot with an offshore crane and rescue helideck stands beyond the fairway. **The anchor falls away** looks through the saloon floor. **Harbor optics** uses the Earth-facing camera. [See the harbor](https://upwell-tetherline.netlify.app/?moment=harbor)

One equatorial tether installation carries several independent climbers. Car 07 grips two guides fore and aft on the upbound side, and passenger and freight cars use a neighboring downbound ribbon. The ship can stop at the nonrotating GEO dock while the colony rotates nearby. The assumed tether continues outboard to a counterweight. The ride models vehicle motion and apparent weight. A structural design would also have to establish tether taper, allowable stress, oscillation under traffic and the counterweight mass. [NASA’s space-elevator design study](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20060000015)

An eighteen-car fleet serves both ends. Normal dispatch is every eight ship hours in each direction. Car 07 meets a regular descent about every four hours aboard, plus the extra supply working F04. This trip encounters five passenger cars, nine freight carriers and two inspection tenders. The **Counterline** page on the saloon console identifies the next working. The cargo cars carry three exposed pallets. T03’s reel and folded inspection arms are visible through its open frame. [Watch the cable tender](https://upwell-tetherline.netlify.app/?moment=tender)

At boarding or under **M → Passage moments**, choose **Guided · 6 minutes** or **Real ship time**. The clock marked **aboard** shows elapsed hours, minutes and seconds. In guided passage the multiplier changes along the route, including slow motion during close passes. At 1×, one ship second takes one second while the ride is running. You can switch pace or jump to a moment midway. Handling a cup and pushing off a handrail use normal time in either mode.

- **Browser** — Select **Board carriage** for the aisle or **Take a window seat** to watch the departure. In the browser, use **WASD** to move and drag to look. Click a fitting or press **E** to operate it. Hold a handle briefly before dragging it. **F** inspects, **I** opens inventory and **Tab** opens the carriage guide. A click, the mouse wheel or the two inspection buttons will turn a deck encoder. **Window views** opens the viewing positions. The altitude readout opens the timetable. **F1** brings up the controls in Unity.

Open **Graphics** or press **F2** while aboard. Windows and Unity WebGL offer **Off** or **FXAA** for antialiasing, cast shadows, atmosphere detail and cabin contact shading. The lighter browser edition also offers the MSAA sample counts supported by its graphics driver and up to **150% supersampling**. The panel shows the actual rendered resolution. Changes take effect while the passage runs. Each player saves its settings for the next ride.

**Moments** beside the altitude display, or **M**, lists thirteen places to rejoin the passage. Use **[** for the previous moment and **]** for the next. The cloud crest starts inside the last bank, twelve seconds before the upper towers clear. Earth’s terminator, orbital sunrise, passing traffic, the receiving bay and the galley bubble each have a viewing position. Turn off **Use suggested view** to keep your camera direction or inspection while the ride continues through the time jump.

Choose **Stargazing window** to sit by the glass during eclipse. The shortcut retracts the solar film, switches off the saloon lamps and turns the display gain down. Guided spends 20 seconds here at real ship time while your eyes adjust. Turn the lights back on to see the two returns in the pressure glazing. At the dock, **Valley gallery** connects to a fixed camera inside the rotating cylinder. Watch the train and waterfront turn beneath the transfer frame. Join the cloud crossing at **1:36**. **Board carriage** puts you at the forward camera where you can follow the opening between the banks down to the Atlantic. **Take a window seat** gives you the same crossing through the pressure glass. Move into the sleeper to see sunlight across the quilt as the car clears the cloud. [Join the cloud crossing](https://upwell-tetherline.netlify.app/?moment=crest)

At **1:12**, look through the shaded side window in the observation saloon. Sunlight briefly reaches the car through a gap. Its shadow crosses the nearby wet cloud, with a faint atmospheric glory around it. Press **O** to face this window, **K** to take a photograph and **J** to see it in the journal. [Take the shaded window](https://upwell-tetherline.netlify.app/?moment=glory) · [NASA aircraft photograph of a glory](https://science.nasa.gov/blogs/earth-matters/2018/07/09/glory-of-the-pilot/)

Pick up the vessel, folio, credential, optics or tool wallet. Drag to turn it, click the moving hardware to operate it, and choose **Stow** to pack it. **Let go** or **G** releases the object with its current motion. **Move & release** lets you move your hand before releasing your grip. You can catch a free item by clicking it. The crew drawer holds a credential for the service hatch. Inside the service lock, four independent tests follow the power, fiber, seal and guide wiring.

The automatic drive reaches 170 m/s (612 km/h). Acceleration rises smoothly to 0.30 m/s². Earth gravity, centrifugal acceleration, braking and Coriolis acceleration determine the apparent load. It begins near 1 g and approaches zero at the synchronous dock. Six minutes of viewing represent about 58.7 hours aboard. Loose-body motion uses passenger seconds so a dropped cup can be followed by eye.

The altitude display shows the current cabin load. At low gravity, **Space** or **Push off** pushes you away from a reachable deck, wall or overhead surface. **X** or **Ctrl** pushes toward the deck. Aim and use **WASD** around the handholds, then release to coast. On touch screens, use **Push off** and **Down**. An unrestrained passenger and previously released objects experience the braking interval when you skip ahead. Braking can leave a flask against the headliner. At the dock, an item released from a still hand stays beside you. **N/P** step through timetable stops and hold at the ends.

Beside the saloon console, the **mechanical accelerometer** has opposed springs and a physical test plunger. Hold the plunger to add 0.10 g to the mechanical reading. The electronic load channel continues to measure the carriage. In the sleeper, the **galley sight glass** contains a fixed charge of dyed water and trapped gas. The primer sets its interface in motion. The shallow-cell model balances acceleration with surface tension and wall wetting, while preserving gas volume through the ground-to-orbit transition. [NASA’s Capillary Flow Experiments](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20090040689)

Try **Press quilt** at the berth, **Brush hem** at the privacy screen, or **Pluck service loop** inside the open inspection cabinet. You can also hold and drag the actual fabric or cable. The mattress supports the loaded quilt. Its restraints hold it as the carriage unloads. Drag the black leading handle along the track and release to leave the screen partway across. The pleats catch the circulating air. The power and fiber loops respond independently between fixed connectors.

The motion study includes a vessel landing on the quilt at the anchor and leaving a passenger’s hand with its spin at the colony. It also shows the proof mass, sight glass, curtain and service loops. [Watch the in-engine demonstration](https://upwell-tetherline.netlify.app/media/cabin-dynamics.mp4)

The service lock’s **Ceramic proof tester** takes a lasting peak reading even if you release the lever early. Hold it through the break at the pier and again near the dock to see the same five pieces move under different loads. Pull the magazine index to seat another strip. In the service lock, **B** looks at the tester, **Q** holds the lever in that view, and **R** indexes the magazine.

On the flight deck, press **LOAD** to see the last 24 seconds and the direction of the apparent weight. Press it again to mark a reading. Mark your weight at the pier, then jump to the galley bubble to watch the sign reverse under braking. At the dock, turn the service handwheel three times. Its enamel dial measures the jaws as they center at 6 mm, draw to 13.5 mm and lock at 18 mm. The sleeve then travels the remaining 2.42 m to its seat.

For a zero-g handling exercise, carry the galley flask to the dock. Move your hand as you let go, push off to catch up with it, and bring it back to the crew sleeper. Approach the counter and choose **Fit in galley cradle** or press **T**. The three spring fingers catch the flask's magnetic heel. Hold the red drinking valve to open the port and let go to close it.

Use **Photo** or **K** at a window, in a room or at an optical camera. **Journal** or **J** opens your six photographs with the view, altitude, passage time and signed cabin load. They are saved in this browser or Windows installation.
